    Tips & reference
    • Triangles are strong. Every span needs diagonal bracing or it will sag.
    • Resonance builds. Several vehicles in a row can make a bridge oscillate.
    • Blue is tension, orange is compression. Force Diagram shows the load paths.
    • Suspension: cable from tower top, down to the hangers, to the deck.
    • Screws: click a beam with the Screw tool to add a joint anywhere along it.
    • Amber, then red, then snap. Watch for red beams before sending something heavier.
    • It is 3D. Try a wind burst, or put a heavy vehicle in the left lane.

    A REAL 3DSTRUCTURE

    You draw one side. The game builds two parallel trusses, joins them with floor beams and bracing, and analyses every bar in three dimensions.

    Bars carry only axial forceF = (EA / L) · elongation

    Every beam is a pin-jointed bar. Positive force is tension, negative is compression.

    Buckling beats yieldingPcr = π² E I / L²

    A long compressed bar fails at Pcr, well below its yield strength. A tube resists far better than a solid square.

    Statics and dynamicsK u = F m a = F

    The report solves the direct stiffness equations. The test runs the dynamics, with progressive collapse when a bar snaps.

    Why bracingsway needs a diagonal

    A pair of side trusses is free to lean sideways. Diagonals in the deck and top planes make each panel rigid.

    Strengths are realistic, but stiffness is scaled down so sag is visible in the game. Vehicles are moving point loads on the deck. Wind, rain and earthquakes are simplified. Not intended for structural design or safety decisions.
